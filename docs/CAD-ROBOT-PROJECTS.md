# Complete CAD robot projects

Open **CAD → Examples** and choose a robot. The main robot cards in
**Shapes → Robots** open the same complete projects; manufacturing parts and
fit coupons remain ordinary insertable shapes. CAD is enabled in Settings.

Each choice uses Brickwright’s shared SB3 loader and restores the assembly,
editable green-flag Blocks, pseudocode source, Arduino Uno circuit, motor-driver
wiring, CAD actuator bindings, and live Widgets together. Confirm replacement
only after saving your current project. Cancelling preserves it. Loading does
not start the program.

After opening, the **Controls** drawer appears. Press **Run**, then move its
sliders. These are the same Widgets that the program reads. **Program → Edit
program** opens the editable program; the Circuit tab shows its actual wiring.
The normal Widgets editor edits these controls. Stop/Reset use the shared
simulation lifecycle. Saving an SB3 retains all these sections.

## Models and behavior

- Scout: left/right TT drive, encoder circuit, two speed controls; starts at 30%.
- Trident: two TT drives and steering servo; centered controls on load.
- Atlas: two wheel drives, a linear lift and fork-tilt servo.
- Mantis: three servo joint controls.
- Cyclone: two linear stages and a sweeper servo.
- Helios: turret and catapult servo controls.

The 22 bundles are generated by `scripts/generate-cad-robot-projects.mjs`
from the committed CAD factory documents. They are not geometry-only presets.
Real controller reporters and device-control blocks connect the program to
Board’s electrical simulation; the coupled mechanical solver owns joint poses.
There are no direct CAD pose assignments in the example programs. Outputs are
updated when a Widget changes, rather than rewriting unchanged pins every tick.

Circuits use nominal 6 V batteries, Uno pin wiring and L293D bridge models.
Continuous TT drives use output-shaft-equivalent constants; linear stages use
nominal bare-rotor constants and an ideal 8 mm/revolution transmission.
These parameters are explicit educational assumptions, not calibrated product
measurements. Positional servos are torque-limited ideal actuators; their
power draw is not modeled. Widgets are simulation inputs, not hardware inputs.
A firmware deployment needs an appropriate hardware input/control program.

## Qualification limits

Browser checks exercise the real GUI, project loader, VM, Widgets, Board and
mechanical worker. Scout, Atlas, Mantis, Cyclone and Helios have passed one-second
simulation checks with every mapped actuator moving and Stop/Reset working.
They do not certify walking gait, sorting success, catapult accuracy or
manufacturing readiness. Trident currently exceeds the bounded simulation time
budget with its detailed colliders; its project remains inspectable and editable,
and the UI labels its simulation experimental.
Solver limits, collision rules and time budgets remain unchanged.
